OUR FOUNDING PATRON
Marshal A. Nesamony,
M.A., B.L. (Ex. M.P.)
--- Page 5 ---
Marshal A Nesamony, a statesman and lawyer, was born on 12
June, 1895 at Nesarpuram in Vilavancode Taluk, Kanyakumari district.
He was educated at Scott Christian High School, Nagercoil and later
graduated from The Maharaja's College, Thiruvananthapuram. He
pursued law at the Law College, Thiruvananthapuram and began his
legal profession in 1921.
Nesamony is remembered for his relentless service to the
downtrodden section of society. He founded the Nagercoil Municipal
Destitute Home and as a member of Travancore-Cochin Town and
Country Planning he played the vital role in developing the rural
areas. Furthermore, he printed and published the Tamil weekly
His Role in Public Life
1943-1947 : Chairman of the Nagercoil Municipality.
1945-1947 : Member of the Travancore Legislative Assembly.
1948-1952 : Member of the Travancore-Cochin Legislative Assembly
and Leader of the Travancore Tamilnadu Congress
Legislative Assembly Party.
1952-1957 : Member of Parliament (Lok Sabha), Nagercoil
Constituency
1954-1957 : President of the Travancore Tamil Nadu Congress Party.
1957-1959 : President of the District Congress Committee.
1956 : Played a pivoted role in the merger of South Travancore
(Kanniyakumari District) with Tamilnadu on 1st November.
1957-1962 : Member of the Tamil Nadu Legislative Assembly.
1957-1962 : Member of the Reform of Local Administration in the
State of Madras.
1962-1968 : Member of Parliament (Lok Sabha), Nagercoil
Constituency.
His role in the KK Diocese of the Church of South India
1933-1947 : Secretary of the Travancore & Mission Church Councils.
1934-1942 : Member of the South District Pastorate Council.
1947-1960 : Vice-President of the South Travancore Diocesan
Council, C.S.I. (Church of South India).
1960-1968 : Vice-President of the K.K. Diocesan Council, C.S.I.
He passed away on 1st June 1968.

1. BRIEF HISTORY OF THE COLLEGE
Nesamony Memorial Christian College is a prestigious
academic and research institution that caters to the students'
academic and personal development, by providing a highly
professional and open-minded education rooted in ethics and values.
The college, sprawled in a lush green campus, has an excellent
infrastructure, library, indoor-outdoor stadium, hostel and internet
facilities. The college offers science, humanities, commerce and
management disciplines with well-equipped department libraries and
classrooms. We have dedicated and erudite faculty members who
help in grooming the students to become world-class citizens in this
globally competitive world.
The college is affiliated to Manonmaniam Sundaranar
University, Tirunelveli, Tamil Nadu and is recognised under 2(f) and
12(B) by the University Grants Commission (UGC), New Delhi.
The 32 acres of the land where the college is located was
bought by Rev. James Emlyn, a missionary from Newcastle UK of the
London Missionary Society. Rev. Robert Sinclair, who succeeded him in
1910, built a bungalow and started a Boarding School.
After independence, the Kanniyakumari Diocese of the
Church of South India took into consideration the long-felt need for a
College at Marthandam and decided to begin a Christian college. The
Executive Committee of the Diocese, which met on 04-01-1964,
entrusted the responsibility of raising funds for the buildings to an
ad-hoc committee consisting of Mr. N. Dennis, Ex. MP(Convener),
Mr. G.A. Ambrose, Mr. J. Nesamony, Mr. P.S. Wilson and Rev. M. Amos.
The Diocese set apart the 32 acres as an endowment to fulfil the
University requirement. The public, in and around Marthandam, the
parents, students and members of the teaching and non-teaching
staff took a keen interest in the growth of the College and contributed
substantially at different stages in its development.
The Pre- University Class (PUC), with the permission of the
Madras University, was started in 1964 with 13 teaching staff members
and 171 students. The College was inaugurated on 6th August 1964 by
the then Bishop Rt. Rev. I.R.H. Gnanadason with Dr. John D.K. Sundar
Singh as the first Principal. Initially, the College was started as a men's
College and it became a Co-Educational institution in the year
1977.The College introduced undergraduate courses with
Mathematics, History and Economics as major subjects in the year
1965. In 1966, the college was affiliated with Madurai University
which was later renamed Madurai Kamaraj University.
--- Page 15 ---
The College became a Post Graduate Institution in the year 1980-81
with the introduction of M.Sc. in Physics. A Ph.D. Research Centre in
History was started in the year 1997 in the regular scheme. The
College now offers 18 U.G., 15 P.G, 12 Ph.D. & 12 Certificate Courses.
Currently, the college has about 3499 students, 201 teaching, 77 non-
teaching staff members and a chaplain.
The College was affiliated to Manonmaniam Sundaranar
University, Tirunelveli in 1991. God has guided and blessed the college
tremendously. With faith in the divine guidance and co-operation
from the public, the College will continue to shine as a fruitful centre
of learning. The College was renamed as Nesamony Memorial
Christian College in recognition of the yeoman services rendered by
the late Thiru. A. Nesamony, B.A.,B.L.,M.P. to the Kanniyakumari
Diocese of the Church of South India in1984.
The College was accredited with 'A' Grade awarded by the
NAAC for the period 2014-2019. The college is ranked in the 42nd
position in the NIRF rankings 2024.
A number of innovative programmes and projects are being
carried out in this college to lift the downtrodden people of this area.
EMBLEM
The emblem of our college bears a Cross, a Church, a Palmyra
tree, a Star, a Book and a Lotus flower. The Cross and the Church
identify the college as a Christian institution and signify sacrifice,
love and holiness. The Palmyra Tree, a distinct tree once the
sustenance of this region symbolises the productive nature of the
institution. The guiding star at the top calls us to guide people
towards light and knowledge. The open Book signifies the liberating
nature of knowledge and the Lotus, our national flower, symbolises
knowledge and wisdom, which the students acquire on campus.
Our Motto- FAITH, ENDEAVOUR, POWER - embraces all these features.
Vision
To make this College a centre with potential for
excellence by imparting quality education to find new paths for
the prosperity and progress of the nation.
Mission
Uplifting and empowering the poor and downtrodden of
this rural and backward area by providing value based and skill
oriented higher education.

2. ADMINISTRATION
Nesamony Memorial Christian College is owned and managed by the
Kanniyakumari Diocese of the Church of South India. The Executive
Committee of the Diocese appoints the Principal of the College. The College
Governing Board, constituted by the Diocese, makes all other appointments
subject to the approval of the Executive Committee. The major financial and
administrative policies of the College are formulated by the Governing Board.
The general administration of the College is vested in the Principal. He is
assisted by the Vice-Principal in the day-to-day administration of the College.
In the absence of the Principal, the Vice-Principal or the authorized Professor
in station shall be in charge of the routine affairs of the College.
--- Page 17 ---
The Principal shall be responsible for supervising the maintenance of the
accounts and properties of the College in accordance with the rules stipulated
by University, Government and Management.
The Head of the departments are responsible for the organization, co-
ordination and smooth running of their departments.

THE STAFF COUNCIL
The Staff Council shall consist of the Principal, Heads of the
Departments, the Senate member, and the Office Superintendent.
The Principal shall be the Chairman. The Council shall appoint one of the
members as the Secretary who shall hold office for one year but shall be
eligible for re-election.
The Staff Council is an advisory body to help the Principal on matters
related to academics and maintenance of discipline. Some of the general
administrative works of the College may be distributed by the Principal among
the members of the Council or other Staff.
The meeting of the Council shall be convened as deemed necessary by
the Principal.
The Secretary of the Council shall keep a record of the proceedings of
all meetings.
The Principal as the Chairman shall be the sole judge of any point of
order.

TUITION FEE
Each student is required to pay the entire fee prescribed for each
semester in a single instalment.
The semester fees for the academic year is collected either before
the reopening of the college or at the time of admission.

December.

tuition fees.

each semester. As per Tamil Nadu Education Rule 90(a), if a student
fails to pay the fee on the designated day, a fine of Rs. 1 per day will be
levied from the date of the fine implementation. If the student fails to
remit the fee with the fine within 10 working days, their name will be
struck off from the college rolls.
The college does not grant exemptions for fee payment,
regardless of leave applications. If readmitted, the student must pay
the outstanding fee, fine, and an additional readmission fee of Rs. 5.

these regulations.

counter. Each borrower is personally responsible for the borrowed

any damage, the borrower must either replace the book or pay for
its cost along with additional charges for overdue items.

providing internet access. Students can utilise this facility free of
charge. Reference books and periodicals are available only for

Article-IV : Election
Mode of Election
The college, abiding by the rules of Manonmaniam Sundaranar
University guidelines (No. MSU/A/CO.IN.EL/2007-08), has
adopted an indirect election method for electing the office
bearers and executive members of the Students' Union Council.
Each class, including undergraduates, postgraduates, and M.Phil.
students, elects a representative. These class representatives
form the electoral college to directly elect the office bearers and
executive members.
Under the proposed system, the apex body will be the
"University Students' Union." The electoral college for the Union
will consist of students' representatives selected from affiliated
colleges and university departments, including a Chairman and
Secretary. Additionally, there will be four office bearers as in
colleges, and two student representatives from each of the
districts Tirunelveli, Tuticorin, and Kanyakumari, resulting in a
total of six representatives for the Executive Committee.

2. The student should have cleared all papers until the date of
nomination.

life.

Representatives Council and the Staff Advisers, shall prepare the
budget for the Council. The budget must be submitted to the Principal
for approval.
It is mandatory for the Council office-bearers to present the
Union's statement of accounts twice per semester to the Students'
Representatives Council.
The office-bearers must obtain the approval and consent of the
Principal before inviting any outsiders to the College for meetings and
functions.
The Council office-bearers shall not engage in any fundraising
campaigns without specific approval from the Principal.
The aforementioned instructions must be followed, and
appropriate action will be taken against those who violate them.

12. CO-CURRICULAR ACTIVITIES
The college offers Curricular, Co-curricular and Extracurricular
activities to enhance campus life and mould students into mature
individuals. Students can enrol their names in committees related to
their interests, whether academic, cultural, sports-oriented, or
community service-focused, enabling them to pursue their passion
and develop leadership skills. NSS/NCC/YRC/SPORTS are part of the
curriculum and it is mandatory for the students to choose one from
the above under PART V. The College offers a range of activities,
including the following:
(a) National Cadet Corps (N.C.C.) (Company III)
The 11th battalion company of the NCC, with its headquarters
in Nagercoil, has a strength of 160 cadets. This mixed battalion
comprises both boys and girls and has been operational for the past
five years. The primary aims of the N.C.C. include:
The main objective of the N.C.C. is to instil in the youth the
qualities of character, courage, comradeship, discipline, leadership,
secular outlook, a spirit of adventure, sportsmanship, and the ideals
of selfless service. Through the cultivation of these values, the N.C.C.
aims to shape the youth into responsible citizens who can make
positive contributions to society.
NCC also aims to develop a well-prepared and motivated group of
young individuals who can take on leadership roles in different fields,
including the armed forces. The idea is to make them consistently
available for national service whenever the need arises.
The N.C.C. training typically spans two years, although it may be
extended for an additional year in exceptional cases. The training
encompasses a range of activities, including drill exercises with or
without arms, weapon training, map reading, bayonet fighting, rifle
and light machine gun firing, elementary military field tactics, and
the study of army organisation and related subjects. Certain cadets
are chosen to participate in special events such as the Annual Training
Camp, Summer Training Camp, Advanced Leadership Training Camp,
Republic Day Parade, Mountaineering Courses, and Attachment to
Army Units, providing them with unique opportunities for further
development.
There will be a total of 40 parades per year, and it is mandatory for
all cadets to attend them. Punctuality, maintaining a neat
appearance, and actively participating with discipline are expected
from every cadet. Based on their proficiency, cadets will have the
opportunity to appear for B and C Certificate examinations.
--- Page 50 ---
(b) National Service Scheme - N.S.S.
There are six N.S.S. units, namely Unit Nos. 82, 83, 135, 139, 188,
and 189, operating under the Youth Welfare Department of
Manonmaniam Sundaranar University. Each unit is composed of 100
authorised student members. The objectives of the N.S.S. are as
follows:
To work with and among people.
To engage in creative and constructive social action.
To make National Service more meaningful and purposeful and
integrate it with the curriculum.
To provide students with an opportunity to participate in nation-
building tasks.
To instil a sense of national consciousness in the minds of the
students.
Enrolment in the N.S.S program is open to degree students with a
positive attitude and a genuine interest in working with and for the
underprivileged. It is important for the students to have a clear
understanding of the objectives and responsibilities associated with
the program. The enrolment process will be completed before 1st
August of every academic year.
A college-level N.S.S committee is established under the
chairmanship of the Principal, with a designated Programme Officer
serving as the Convenor. The committee consists of no more than 10
members, including senior staff members, students, and community
members interested in the program. This Advisory Committee
collaborates to plan the activities and programs for the academic
year, and a minute book is maintained to record the proceedings of
the committee.
The N.S.S. program officers will provide further details regarding
the projects and programs.
Unnat Bharat Abhiyan (UBA) - Village Adoption
As part of the Unnat Bharat Abhiyan (UBA), NMCC has adopted the
villages of Alancode near Vavarai, Kannanvilai near Kollenchi,
Mankalakuntu near Midalam in Killiyoor Taluk, Kumarankudi, and
Surulacode near Thiruvattar in Thiruvattar Taluk. These villages face
significant social and economic challenges and are considered
underprivileged in terms of development. The objectives behind
adopting these villages are as follows:
To conduct community development programs.
To encourage adult education and literacy programs.
To conduct environmental awareness programs.
To conduct social work camps with the help of NGOs.
To make the people aware of government welfare schemes.
To organise health and hygiene awareness camps.
To provide education to the local people on numerous rights.
To create awareness among the people about consumer problems.

( c) Youth Red Cross
Youth Red Cross unit is a platform that allows young students to play
an active role in humanitarian efforts. The objective is to expand
their knowledge and develop skills that will be beneficial not only for
their academic journey but also for their future careers as responsible
global citizens.
The Youth Red Cross' primary objective is to educate and train
student volunteers to provide humanitarian services and address the
health needs of the sick and suffering. Furthermore, it encourages
students to donate blood voluntarily. The aim is to involve college
students in humanitarian activities, develop a sense of volunteerism,
and increase awareness about health and social issues.
YRC conducts health awareness programs and workshops that cover
topics such as sanitation, hygiene, nutrition, and disease prevention.
The benefits of these initiatives extend beyond students to the wider
community. Their mission is to educate both students and the
community by organizing awareness campaigns on significant social
issues, which may include topics such as HIV/AIDS prevention or
environmental conservation. YRC provides college students with
essential first aid training, which will enable them to respond
effectively to medical emergencies in their communities and beyond.
(d) Sports
Sports help students to stay physically fit and healthy. Students can
improve their skills for their personal growth and future career
opportunities. They promote physical and mental well-being,
encourage social interaction, and contribute to personal
development. The sports committee plays a crucial role in boosting
the college's sports culture. They organise a variety of events, inter-
college tournaments, and friendly matches. These events not only let
students display their skills and strengthen bonds within the college
but also provide chances to represent the college at the university
level, offering greater recognition and opportunities in the sports
world.
Enrolment
Enrolment is open to the UG students under Part V in the
curriculum. It is important for the students to have a clear
understanding of the objectives and responsibilities associated with
the programmes. The enrolment process will be completed in the
month of August. A college-level committee is established under the
chairmanship of the Principal, with a dedicated team of staff
members.

14. ASSOCIATIONS AND CLUBS
The Clubs and Associations promote holistic development by
fostering leadership skills, critical thinking, through various
activities. They encourage collaboration, interpersonal skills, and a
sense of community, benefiting both students and staff members in
their personal growth and skill-building journey.
The Principal serves as the Patron of all Associations, while the
Head of the Department relevant to the subject holds the position of
the President. The position of the Vice-President is held by the next
Senior Professor in the Department. The Secretary of the Association
is elected from among the final year class representatives.
The President and Vice-President of the associations (other
than Department Associations) are elected from among the teaching
staff members. The Secretaries are chosen from the student body
through elections conducted, by representatives of the respective
classes.
The primary goal of each Association is to actively support
students in their academic endeavours, nurture leadership qualities,
and facilitate their development as responsible citizens The elected
office-bearers of the Associations are responsible for organizing
engaging activities such as debates and guest lectures relevant to
their respective subjects. It is mandatory that all programs and
initiatives receive approval from the Presidents of the respective
Associations and the Principal to ensure that they align with the
college's overall goals and values.
The Secretary of each Association, in collaboration with the
other office-bearers and the President, is responsible for devising the
program plan and budget for the academic year. It is essential to
ensure that the expenditure related to the activities of each
association does not surpass the allocated amount provided by the
Principal.
